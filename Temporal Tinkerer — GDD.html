Story Premise

The player receives a letter from their uncle — a tinkerer and amateur inventor — bubbling with excitement about a device he's finally gotten working. They drive out to his house. The front door is ajar. Inside: overturned furniture, drawers pulled out, papers everywhere. The uncle is gone.

The device is not in the house. Tobias left it in a trunk with Agnes Mae Whitaker in MillHaven, a separate town through the woods, and the key to the trunk arrives with his letter. Once the player has earned Agnes's trust and opened the trunk, the device takes them back two days, to the last day Tobias sat at his desk. The house is intact and the uncle is already gone, but everything he laid out for anyone who came looking is still there. The clues lead to 1947, when the Ashworth deed was recorded, and to June 1962, when the workshop burned. Tobias is stuck in 1962, and the player has to get him out.

✓ Locked: The uncle's first name is Tobias. Appears in the opening letter, journal pages, and town records.

Opening Hook & Tutorial

✓ Locked: The device is not hidden in the ransacked house. It is in a trunk Tobias left with Agnes Mae Whitaker in MillHaven, a separate town; the key to the trunk arrives in the opening letter. The house holds other clues and puzzles instead.

Arrival Sequence

  1. A mail truck delivers the uncle's letter. The player takes it from the mailbox and reads it in the journal. It names Agnes Mae Whitaker in MillHaven and encloses the trunk key.
  2. First scene: front of the house. The door stands open and something is wrong.
  3. Explore the ransacked house: the stopped foyer clock (11:47), two photographs on the living-room mantel, and a trail note in the study that explains the forest signs.
  4. Solve the forest signs (every third word, first letter) to find the route to MillHaven.
  5. In MillHaven, earn Agnes's trust with the letter and the key, open her attic trunk, and take the time device and Tobias's second letter, which explains the device and gives the date Nov 7, 2026.

Tutorial: 48 Hours Ago

The player travels to Nov 7, 2026. The house is intact and warm, and the uncle is already gone. His workspace is laid out exactly as he left it. No pressure, no time limit. This space teaches:

  • How to use the device and the Known Dates list
  • How to navigate rooms and examine objects (the desk calendar, the corkboard, the wall safe)
  • How the journal records every clue and date
  • How to return home (the device's Home date, Nov 9, 2026)

The calendar (March 15, 1947) and the corkboard note give the safe combination, 31547. In the safe are a photograph of the deed and the workshop key. The player gets the rules of the game through the uncle's own handwriting, not through a tutorial screen.

✓ Locked: The first journey is deliberate: the player travels to Nov 7, 2026 with the device from the trunk. The past tutorial space is 48 hours ago, house intact, uncle absent.

The Mystery

The Core Conflict

The uncle discovered that the county's record of the Ashworth deed was falsified. In 1947 Rutherford Ashworth deeded the land to the family and the deed was recorded at County Records. In 1962 Cornelius Montclair, who wanted the estate for himself, had the county's page replaced with a forgery notarized by his bride, Constance Vance. The original deed vanished from the vault. The uncle went back to prove it, and he never came home.

✓ Locked: Antagonist family name: Montclair. The Montclair name appears on the town hall facade, library wing, and a commercial building on main street. In 1947, those same buildings bear the original owner's name — a visible before/after the player witnesses across eras.

Key Historical Event

Cornelius Montclair married Constance Vance on Saturday, June 9, 1962, and had her sign papers “for the house” the week before. On the night of Thursday, June 14, 1962, the Estate workshop at Ashworth Hollow burned, and Tobias was carried off to the cellar of the Ashworth house. His device was damaged, so he was stuck in June 1962. The player finds him on the morning of June 15, with the original deed taken from the 1947 County Records vault as proof.

The Time Travel Device

Fiction & Form

The device is a wearable brass instrument — the size of a large watch — that the uncle spent years building. Its face, when summoned as a UI popup, reveals a small mechanical instrument panel: three input windows (Month, Day, Year), a Known Dates log, and a Travel lever. It is powered by mechanisms the uncle never fully explained, only documented.

Key Design Rules

  • Any date works (revised): The device never rejects a date. A key date takes the player to that era; any other date takes them to an empty "elsewhere" screen (see Key Dates & Travel). The player still has to discover the key dates, so knowledge remains the gate.
  • Known Dates log: Every discovered date is listed in the drawer. Selecting one auto-fills the dials. This is the power-user path; first-time players enjoy dialing manually.
  • Items travel with you (revised): What you carry stays in the bag when you change era. The journal records everything significant. Items are used on people from the bag: choose Look in my bag in a conversation, pick the item, then click the person.
  • Talking in the past (revised): People in the past talk to the player (Eleanor, Amos, Constance, Tobias and others), and some only talk once the player has earned their trust. Conversations alternate their line, the player's line, their line. Look in my bag appears only while the person is expecting something; if you are holding what they are waiting for, it is the only choice besides Leave.

Key Dates & Travel

✓ Locked (revision): Whatever date the player enters, the device takes them somewhere. Key dates go to the real location in that era. Every other date goes to a screen with no hotspots and a Back function.

How a Date Is Resolved

  1. Key-date table first. If the entered date falls inside a key date's window, travel to that era's real scene.
  2. Era bucket second. Otherwise use the year-range bucket and show its "elsewhere" image with no hotspots.
  3. Back / Previous. From an elsewhere screen the player returns to the last real location they left. The device button still works there for a second jump.

Fuzzy Windows

The player does not have to hit the exact day. If the month and year are right, a window of about a week (default ±3 days) works the same. Some entries are flagged exact where the story needs it.

  • A window must never cross a change of state. The workshop fire window ends on June 14, because June 15 shows the burned workshop.
  • The date the player typed is stored (see below). The scene shown is the key date's scene.

The Date as a Variable

  • The date the player entered is stored as a variable (for example TravelManager.EnteredDate) and is available to any script.
  • An NPC asked for the date says back the date the player entered, in an era-appropriate form.
  • An NPC can answer "what's the date?" by handing the player a new date, which goes into Known Dates and can start the next trip.
  • NPC lines should depend on the era's state (before or after an event), not the exact day, so a window never lets someone mention something that has not happened yet.
✓ Locked: Windows, exact-date flags and the era pictures for non-key dates are final, as listed above. Changing the day inside the same era reloads the scene, so each day shows its own pictures (June 12 and June 13 look different at the Hollow).

Core Loop

Every puzzle in the game is built from one repeating structure:

1
Discovery

Find a clue in the present (newspaper, gravestone, letter, journal) that reveals a specific date.

2
Preparation

Understand what you're looking for before you travel. The clue tells you what existed at that place, before it was destroyed or removed.

3
Exploration

Arrive in the past. The room looks different — objects present, intact, legible. Find the thing you couldn't reach in the present.

4
Application

Return to the present. Use what you learned. Sometimes a past clue raises a new question — chaining loops is the mid-game escalation.

What Crosses Time

  • Knowledge always — journal auto-records everything significant. The player carries full memory.
  • Items never — no physical objects cross eras. No inventory paradoxes. No key from 1962 opens a 2026 lock directly.
  • Exceptions (late-game twist only) — one or two story-significant moments where the rule bends, deliberate and narrative-justified.

Woods Navigation System

Structure

The woods are genuinely navigable only once the player finds the uncle's hand-drawn map — located in the past (48 hours ago) pinned to the study wall. In the present, it's gone. Reading the map in the past auto-records it to the journal. Without the journal entry, every path loops.

Hidden Sequence Logic

The game tracks the player's fork choices silently — never displayed, never counted. After each fork, it checks:

  • Workshop path match (L R L R L R L L R) → Workshop Exterior scene
  • Town path match (R L R L R L R R L) → Town Entrance scene
  • Still viable (can still match either path) → Continue normally
  • Diverged from both → Enter wander mode

Wander Mode

When the sequence is broken, the player doesn't snap back immediately. They continue through 3–6 more procedural straight/fork scenes before the path naturally loops back to the meadow entrance. Wander length is randomly varied each time — it's uncountable. The sequence resets silently on meadow re-entry.

Atmosphere as Soft Signal

StateVisual treatmentImplementation
Correct pathCanopy thins, birdsong clearer, light slightly warmerGodot: lighter color modulation, birdsong audio layer louder
Wander modeDenser canopy, cooler palette, same birdsong quieterGodot: slight blue-shift modulation, reduced audio

This isn't a puzzle clue — it's atmosphere. Players feel the difference in hindsight after finding the map, not before.

Woods Map Puzzle (Core Mechanic Showcase)

The map puzzle is a perfect showcase of the core loop: the navigation information the player needs exists only in the past. Finding it in the past converts the woods from obstacle to scenery in one satisfying moment. First-time players who rush the woods get the Zelda II experience; explorers who check the study first get a path that works.

The Town

The town is MillHaven (sign: EST. 1887), a completely separate town from Ashworth Hollow. The Montclair name appears on its town hall facade, a library wing, and a commercial building on main street. These architectural details must be visible in the town exterior art.

✓ Locked — Ashworth Hollow naming: The Hollow began as a settlement supporting the estate's workers, so its buildings are named for what they were used for, not for a family: Estate Workshop, Caretaker's Cottage, Estate Icehouse, Hollow Mill, and the Notice Board. The Montclair name appears only on MillHaven's civic and commercial buildings, never on the Hollow's. The Hollow keeps its working names while Montclair puts its name on the town.

Key Town Scenes

The town hub has six buildings, each reached by a click on the hub picture.

  • Agnes's house: Agnes Mae Whitaker keeps Tobias's trunk in the attic. In 2026 she is the gate to the story and every reveal comes through her: trust, the first half of the story, the whole story, the logbook and, last, the date June 15, 1962.
  • Historical Society: Reginald Ames. The 2026 archive has the 1962 volume with June cut out. In 1962 the same building is the library wing, with the Gazette archive in its basement: the volumes 1950–1961, the crate with the 1962 volume, and the June 11 report of the Montclair wedding.
  • The empty storefront: Walter Pritchard's father did the library renovations in June 1962. His ledger on the stool dates the night the bound Gazettes were moved: June 12.
  • County Records: Marjorie Hale (2026) and Mr. Pruitt (1947). Deed Book 14 holds the forged page; the original deed is in vault box nine on March 21, 1947.
  • The church: The Reverend and the 1962 marriage register: Constance Vance, aged 21, and Agnes as a witness.
  • The Harwick Diner: Dolores (2026) names Eleanor Reid. In 1962 Eleanor, the young night waitress, is the witness: she saw Cornelius's Cadillac come off the Hollow road with its headlights off on June 14. She talks only once the player earns her trust.
✓ Locked: Tobias's fate. He is held in the cellar of the Ashworth house after the fire and is stuck in June 1962 because the device was damaged. The player frees him on June 15, 1962 and brings him home to Nov 9, 2026.

Art Direction

Style

Photorealistic 3D render quality. Matches the bonus game's visual language — not illustrated, not painted. Sharp material detail, strong three-layer depth (foreground / midground / background), single dominant warm light source per scene, rich naturalistic color. No people, no text, no UI intrusion.
